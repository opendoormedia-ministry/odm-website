<p>A Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) puts your organization on Google Search and Google Maps so people can find you, see your hours, read reviews, and contact you — for free. This guide walks you through creating and verifying your profile from scratch.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Part 1: Sign In and Start Your Profile</h2>
<ol><li>Go to <a href="https://business.google.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">business.google.com</a> and click <strong>Manage now</strong>.</li><li>Sign in with a Google account you want associated with this business. If you don't have one, click <strong>Create account</strong> and follow the prompts.<ul><li>Use a business-owned Google account if possible (e.g., one tied to your organization's Gmail), rather than a personal account.</li></ul></li></ol>
<hr>
<h2>Part 2: Enter Your Business Details</h2>
<ol><li>Type your business name in the search field. If it appears in the dropdown, your profile may already exist — click it and follow the prompts to claim it. Otherwise, click <strong>Add your business to Google</strong>.</li><li>Choose the <strong>business category</strong> that best describes what you do (e.g., "Nonprofit organization," "Photographer," "Church"). You can add more categories later.</li><li>When asked <em>"Do you want to add a location customers can visit?"</em>:<ul><li>Select <strong>Yes</strong> if you have a physical storefront, office, or venue people come to.</li><li>Select <strong>No</strong> if you're service-area based (e.g., you travel to clients).</li></ul></li><li>If you selected <strong>Yes</strong>, enter your full street address.</li><li>If you selected <strong>No</strong> (or in addition to a physical location), you'll be asked to define your <strong>service area</strong> — add the cities, counties, or regions you serve.</li><li>Enter your <strong>business phone number</strong> and <strong>website URL</strong> (if you have one). If your site isn't live yet, you can leave the website field blank and add it later.</li><li>Click <strong>Next</strong> to continue.</li></ol>
<hr>
<h2>Part 3: Verify Your Business</h2>
<p>Google requires verification before your profile goes live. The method offered to you depends on your business type and location. Common options include:</p>
<table><thead><tr><th>Method</th><th>How It Works</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><strong>Postcard</strong></td><td>Google mails a postcard with a 5-digit code to your business address. This usually takes 5–14 days. Enter the code at business.google.com when it arrives.</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Phone / Text</strong></td><td>Google calls or texts a verification code to your business phone number. Enter it immediately.</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Email</strong></td><td>Google sends a verification link to the email on file. Click it to verify.</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Video recording</strong></td><td>For some business types, Google may request a short video showing your location, signage, or equipment. Follow the on-screen instructions.</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Live video call</strong></td><td>A Google representative will join a call to verify your business in real time. Schedule it when prompted.</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Select the method available to you and follow the prompts. Your profile will remain in a limited "pending verification" state until the code is confirmed.</p>
<blockquote><strong>Tip:</strong> If you're waiting on a postcard, you can still fill out your profile in the meantime — add photos, hours, and a description — it just won't be publicly visible until verification is complete.</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2>Part 4: Complete Your Profile</h2>
<p>Once verified (or while you wait), fill in as much detail as possible. A complete profile ranks better and earns more trust.</p>
<ul><li><strong>Business hours</strong> — including any special holiday hours</li><li><strong>Description</strong> — a brief summary of what you do and who you serve (750 characters max)</li><li><strong>Photos</strong> — a logo, a cover photo, and images of your space, team, or work</li><li><strong>Services or products</strong> — list what you offer</li><li><strong>Attributes</strong> — things like "wheelchair accessible," "veteran-owned," "nonprofit," etc.</li></ul>
